# Journals for qualitative and multi-method political science

Read by `/qualstack:journal-fit` to build and score the candidate set, and by
`/qualstack:referee-mock` (editor persona) for the target journal's row, the
fit signals, and the desk-reject risks. It lists candidates and fit signals,
not current policies: word limits, appendix rules, data and transparency
policies, open-access rules, and turnaround change, so they are always
fetched from the journal's current author guidelines (the skill does this
with WebFetch and WebSearch). A journal's last three years of articles and
its current editors' statement overrule the orientations below.

## Candidate outlets by venue type

Orientation, not ranking: read the subfield's table, the regional table when
the cases sit in one region, and Methods only when the method is the point.

### General political science

| Journal | What it publishes that matters for fit | Receptivity to case-based, process-tracing, multi-method work | Fit signals to look for |
|---|---|---|---|
| American Political Science Review (APSR) | APSA flagship; every subfield and method; the contribution must matter beyond one subfield | Publishes qualitative and multi-method articles, a minority of its content; touchstones of qualitative method appeared here: Mahoney & Goertz (2004), Lieberman (2005), Humphreys & Jacobs (2015) | Recent articles whose cases carry the inference rather than illustrate it; the current editorial team's statement; a general claim on page one |
| American Journal of Political Science (AJPS) | MPSA generalist with a strong quantitative, formal, and methods orientation | Case-based articles are uncommon; multi-method papers whose cases test the mechanism behind a quantitative result are the likelier route | Recent multi-method articles; whether replication materials are verified before publication and how qualitative and confidential evidence is handled |
| Journal of Politics (JOP) | SPSA generalist, political theory included | Mostly quantitative and formal; case-based empirical articles are uncommon | Multi-method articles in the subfield; any data-deposit requirement and how it treats interviews and archival sources |
| British Journal of Political Science (BJPS) | Generalist with strong comparative and European coverage | Mostly quantitative; publishes some case-based and multi-method articles | A comparative argument with cross-national reach; recent qualitative articles in the debate |
| Perspectives on Politics | APSA's broad, integrative, problem-driven journal; also review essays and book reviews | Receptive to qualitative, historical, interpretive, and multi-method work; published the Qualitative Transparency Deliberations synthesis, Jacobs et al. (2021) | An argument addressed to the whole discipline in accessible prose; light method exposition; the current editors' statement of what a research article must do |
| Political Research Quarterly (PRQ) | WPSA generalist; methodologically pluralist | Publishes qualitative and multi-method work alongside quantitative; Seawright & Gerring (2008) appeared here | Recent articles in the subfield; a realistic generalist when the top three are a reach |
| Polity | NPSA generalist; theory, American, comparative, and IR | Open to qualitative, historical, and theoretical work | Recent articles in the subfield and debate |

### Comparative politics

| Journal | What it publishes that matters for fit | Receptivity to case-based, process-tracing, multi-method work | Fit signals to look for |
|---|---|---|---|
| World Politics | Comparative politics and IR theory; long research articles and review articles | Regularly publishes comparative-historical, process-tracing, and multi-method articles; Collier & Mahoney (1996), Collier & Levitsky (1997), and Capoccia & Kelemen (2007) appeared here | A theoretical argument with broad comparative reach; recent case-based articles in the same debate |
| Comparative Political Studies (CPS) | Broad comparative journal; high volume; special issues | Publishes qualitative, comparative-historical, and multi-method articles alongside quantitative work; Slater & Ziblatt (2013) appeared here | Recent articles on the region and debate; its policy on interview data |
| Comparative Politics | Theory-driven comparative articles grounded in country knowledge | Case-based and small-N comparative articles are common | Case expertise plus a concept or mechanism that travels |
| Studies in Comparative International Development (SCID) | Development, political economy, and state-society relations | Publishes subnational and small-N comparisons and multi-method work; Snyder (2001) appeared here | A development-relevant outcome; the regional balance of recent volumes |
| Government and Opposition | Parties, oppositions, regimes, and democracy, comparatively | Pluralist; case studies and small-N comparisons appear alongside quantitative work | Recent articles on the regime type or party family |
| Democratization | Democratization, autocracy, hybrid regimes, transitions | Publishes single-case and small-N studies as well as quantitative work | Precise regime concepts, per Collier & Levitsky (1997); recent articles in the debate |
| Journal of Democracy | Hybrid policy and academic journal; short, accessible essays for scholars and practitioners | Not a venue for methods-forward process tracing; a distilled argument from completed research can fit | How submissions are solicited and reviewed; suits the practitioner version once the research article is placed |
| Comparative Studies in Society and History (CSSH) | Multidisciplinary comparative journal (history, anthropology, sociology) | Historical and interpretive work is the norm; reviewers may be historians or anthropologists | Engagement with historiography and with the case on its own terms |

### Methods

| Journal | What it publishes that matters for fit | Receptivity to case-based, process-tracing, multi-method work | Fit signals to look for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sociological Methods & Research (SMR) | Methods journal: publishes methodological contributions, not substantive case studies | Publishes qualitative and set-theoretic methodology; Mahoney (2012) on process-tracing tests appeared here | A general procedure, demonstrated, with its failure conditions |
| Political Analysis | Society for Political Methodology journal; methods contributions | Mostly quantitative methodology; Geddes (1990), Fairfield & Charman (2017), and Zaks (2017) appeared here | A procedure, software, or simulation that applies beyond one case |
| Political Science Research and Methods (PSRM) | EPSA journal; substantive and methods research with a design emphasis | Qualitative and multi-method designs appear but are uncommon | A multi-method design innovation |
| PS: Political Science & Politics | APSA's journal of the profession; short articles and symposia | Has published widely used practical guides: Berry (2002), Moravcsik (2010), Collier (2011), Fujii (2012) | A teachable procedure or a professional-norms argument, not a substantive finding |
| Qualitative & Multi-Method Research (QMMR) | The publication of APSA's Qualitative and Multi-Method Research section; symposia and short methodological essays | Speaks to the section's audience; check its current format, review process, and whether it takes unsolicited pieces | A short reflection or symposium contribution, not a full article |
| Journal of Mixed Methods Research | Interdisciplinary mixed-methods methodology rooted in education, health, and evaluation research | Its design typologies (sequential, convergent) differ from political science's nested analysis and Bayesian integration | A methods contribution that translates between the two vocabularies |

## Fit signals specific to qualitative work

Code the last three years of research articles (skip editorials, symposia,
and book reviews) before scoring; the coding beats remembered reputation.

- **Case-based share and placement.** Code each article's method
  (quantitative, formal, case-based, multi-method, historical, interpretive)
  and evidence (archives, interviews, fieldwork, secondary). None case-based
  or multi-method in three years caps method receptivity at 2; cases pushed
  to appendices mean a process-tracing table, not a narrative, in the text.
- **Evidence appendices.** Whether recent qualitative articles carry
  evidence tables, source lists, interview-methods tables (Bleich & Pekkanen
  2013), active citation (Moravcsik 2010), Annotation for Transparent Inquiry
  (ATI), or Qualitative Data Repository (QDR) deposits. If so, reviewers
  expect them at submission: draft `paper/sections/appendix.tex` with
  `/qualstack:draft-section` and stage the deposit with `/qualstack:archive`.
- **Formal qualitative inference.** Whether the journal has published
  Bayesian process tracing (Fairfield & Charman 2017) or causal-model work
  (Humphreys & Jacobs 2023; Tietz et al. 2026). If not, explain the model,
  priors, and query in prose, move code and `data/case-data.csv` to the
  replication files, and expect a reviewer new to the method.
- **Editors, board, and likely reviewers.** Qualitative methodologists or
  specialists on the paper's region among the editors raise receptivity;
  teams rotate, so date the statement you read. Authors of recent articles
  on the case or debate are the reviewer pool: area experts press on
  sources, methodologists on case selection and inference.
- **Transparency policy.** Fetch it: qualitative deposit required,
  encouraged, or silent; the exemption route for confidential human-subjects
  data; pre-publication verification. Never assume: positions moved with
  JETS (2014), DA-RT, and the Qualitative Transparency Deliberations
  (Jacobs et al. 2021; Elman, Kapiszewski & Lupia 2018), and journals differ
  on interview data protected under APSA (2020).
- **Length norms.** Set the words the case narrative needs (from
  `paper/sections/cases.tex` or the `/qualstack:chronology` memos) against
  the fetched cap and what counts toward it (notes, references, appendices).
  Letters and short formats rarely hold case evidence; use them only when
  the qualitative component can sit in the appendix.
- **Book or article.** An argument needing several full case narratives at
  archival depth is a book; say so, and score the article version: the
  theory plus one decisive case, or the cross-case pattern plus compressed
  evidence. Check the press's policy on prior publication of chapters;
  short-monograph series (Cambridge Elements and similar) sit between.
- **The conversation.** Where the must-engage works in `.qualstack/lit-map.md`
  appeared, and whether the journal published on the case, region, or debate
  in the last five years; if nothing in its recent pages bears on the
  argument, fit is low however strong the method.

## Matching paper type to venue

Read the row for `design.type` in `.qualstack/config.yaml`; the interview and
archival rows apply when those `type` values dominate `evidence/log.csv`.

| Paper type | Realistic venue types | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Single-case process tracing with a formal model (`process-tracing`) | Subfield flagships that publish case-based theory testing (World Politics, IO, International Security, CPS); generalists with qualitative traffic (APSR, Perspectives, PRQ) when the case is of general interest; area or topic journals when the case is the stake; a methods journal only if the procedure is new | The model makes the single-case inference explicit, which meets the editor's first objection; a population claim still needs priors from beyond the case (Humphreys & Jacobs 2023) |
| Small-N comparison (`comparative`) | Comparative flagships (World Politics, CPS, Comparative Politics); development and regime journals (SCID, Democratization, Government and Opposition); regional journals | Controlled comparison is native there (Slater & Ziblatt 2013); generalists ask why not more cases unless the comparison isolates what a large-N cannot |
| Nested or multi-method: large-N plus cases (`multi-method`) | Generalists (APSR, AJPS, JOP, BJPS); subfield flagships (CPS, IO, ISQ); PSRM when the integration is the design innovation; field journals where the topic is core | The large-N meets generalists' expectations; the cases earn space by testing the mechanism or measurement the regressions cannot (Lieberman 2005; Seawright 2016); the cap decides whether they stay in the main text |
| Comparative-historical (`comparative-historical`) | World Politics; Politics & Society; SAPD for U.S. development; CPS, SCID, Comparative Politics, CSSH; a book when sequences span several cases | Long-run arguments need length; these venues accept historiography-based evidence when source selection is explicit (Lustick 1996) and speak the language of junctures and sequences (Capoccia & Kelemen 2007; Falleti & Mahoney 2015) |
| Interview-based elite study (`interview` evidence) | Comparative and area journals (Comparative Politics, CPS, African Affairs, Post-Soviet Affairs, LAPS); RIPE, SER, or Politics & Society for economic elites; FPA or Security Studies for policymakers | These value access and context; reviewers check sampling, saturation, and reporting (Berry 2002; Bleich & Pekkanen 2013); confirm a confidential-data route before submitting |
| Archival diplomatic history (`archival` evidence) | Historians' journals (Diplomatic History, JCWS); history-friendly security journals (International Security, Security Studies, JSS); IO or ISQ when the case tests a general IR theory | Historians judge the archival and historiographic contribution (Trachtenberg 2006), political scientists the theoretical payoff (Thies 2002); the same research needs two framings |
| Methods contribution about qualitative inference | Political Analysis; SMR; PSRM; APSR or Perspectives for discipline-wide arguments; PS or QMMR for short practical pieces | These publish procedures, not findings: a general method, a demonstration, and the conditions under which it fails (Mahoney 2012; Humphreys & Jacobs 2015; Fairfield & Charman 2017) |

## Desk-reject risks for qualitative submissions

Each risk that applies lowers the desk-reject score and becomes a comment
from the `/qualstack:referee-mock` editor.

- **An n of 1 framed as a general claim.** One case shows how the outcome
  arose there; a population claim needs a model linking case to population,
  scope conditions, or a cross-case component (Humphreys & Jacobs 2023;
  Gerring 2017). Answer "a case of what?" on page one.
- **No stated case-selection logic.** Name the strategy against the query
  and population (typical, deviant, most- or least-likely, diverse;
  Seawright & Gerring 2008), defend any selection on the outcome (Geddes
  1990; Collier & Mahoney 1996), and write it with `/qualstack:case-select`.
- **Thick description without a question.** No query, no rival, and no
  evidence that would have counted against the argument.
- **Length far beyond norms.** Over-cap manuscripts can come back unread;
  move the chronology and evidence tables to the appendix, or cut a case.
- **Missing transparency appendix.** Where the journal's recent qualitative
  articles carry evidence tables, interview-methods tables, or ATI, leaving
  them out tells the editor the norms were not read.
- **Wrong register.** A substantive paper at a methods journal; hypotheses
  tested on secondary sources at a historians' journal; narrative history
  without a theoretical payoff at a political science journal.
- **Area paper without a portable lesson at a generalist.** Page one says
  what a reader who does not study the country learns.
- **Test labels without probative value.** "Smoking gun" or "hoop" with no
  account of why the evidence is unlikely under the rival (Van Evera 1997;
  Collier 2011; Bennett 2015) reads as inference claimed, not shown.
- **Anonymization and ethics failures.** Fieldwork narration that unblinds
  the author; informants identifiable from position, place, and date; no
  human-subjects statement where required (APSA 2020). Check the manuscript
  and appendix with `/qualstack:deidentify` and `/qualstack:submission-pack`.
- **A book chapter submitted as an article.** Cross-references to other
  chapters, or a theory that needs the rest of the book.

## Scoring dimensions

Fetch every cap, policy, and turnaround figure first; record date and page.

| Dimension | Scale | Qualitative-specific note |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | 1–5 | Generalist vs. specialist reach; citation metrics understate area and history journals in their own communities; ask what the user's department counts before scoring an area journal low |
| Fit | 1–5 | Nothing on the case, region, or debate in five years = 1–2; where the must-engage works in `.qualstack/lit-map.md` appeared = 4–5; a journal in the other register (historians' journal for a hypothesis-testing paper, methods journal for a substantive one) = 1 |
| Method receptivity | 1–5 | From the recent-volume coding: no case-based or multi-method articles in three years = 1–2; occasional = 3; regular, with explicit tests, Bayesian process tracing, or causal-model work = 4–5 |
| Length fit | 1–5 | Words the case narrative needs vs. the fetched cap and what counts toward it (notes, references, appendix); 1 when fitting means cutting a case the argument depends on |
| Review turnaround | 1–5 | Posted median to first decision when the journal publishes one, otherwise "unknown", never a guess; weigh against the chance of a revise-and-resubmit that asks for another archive trip or interview round |
| Desk-reject risk | 1–5 (5 = low) | The editor's stated criteria plus the risks above that apply; a substantive paper at a methods journal = 1 |
| Transparency and replication policy | note | Qualitative deposit required, encouraged, or silent; the exemption route for confidential data and what it requires; evidence-appendix, active-citation, or ATI expectations; pre-publication verification; set against the `confidential` and `archive-restricted` rows of `evidence/log.csv` |

- **Tiers.** Reach: highest impact with fit and method receptivity ≥ 3.
  Realistic: highest fit × method receptivity × low desk-reject risk.
  Backup: low desk-reject risk, length fit ≥ 4, a policy the evidence meets.
- **Policy gate.** No journal enters a tier while its policy has no route for
  the paper's confidential evidence; if the policy is ambiguous, draft a
  pre-submission query to the editor naming the constraint.
- **Lead time.** The deposit (`/qualstack:archive`) and the anonymization and
  format sweep (`/qualstack:submission-pack`) take weeks; start both now.
